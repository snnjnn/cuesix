# Product Guidelines: cuesix

## 1. Prose Style & Tone

All project documentation, including `READMEs`, architectural documents, and release notes, must adhere to a **Technical and Precise** prose style.

-   **Accuracy First:** Prioritize technical accuracy above all else.
-   **Formal Language:** Use formal, unambiguous language. Avoid jargon where a simpler term exists, but do not shy away from precise technical terminology when necessary.
-   **Detail-Oriented:** Provide comprehensive details and clear, factual explanations. Assume the reader is an engineering professional who values accuracy.

## 2. Brand Messaging

The core message of `cuesix` revolves around its role as a stable, developer-focused tool for APISIX management.

### 2.1 Primary Messaging Pillars

-   **Stability and Reliability:** All external communications should reinforce the idea that `cuesix` is a robust, production-ready tool that prevents configuration errors and ensures uptime. We are a safeguard, not an experiment.
-   **Developer Experience:** Frame `cuesix` as a tool that simplifies the lives of DevOps engineers and developers. Highlight its role in improving workflows, enabling configuration-as-code, and providing clear feedback.

### 2.2 Messaging Examples

-   **For Release Notes:** "This release further enhances the validation engine, providing even greater assurance against misconfigurations."
-   **For Public Documentation:** "By integrating `cuesix` into your CI/CD pipeline, you can create a seamless, automated workflow for managing APISIX configurations with confidence."

## 3. Visual Identity

As a backend service, `cuesix` does not have a visual identity. All branding is expressed through its messaging and the quality of its implementation.
